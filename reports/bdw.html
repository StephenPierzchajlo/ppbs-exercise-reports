Belief in a dangerous world

Study characteristics

This report covers Belief in a dangerous world: 500 respondents linked by exercise_id across 5 waves (1 to 5), measured with 5 items on a 1 to 7 response scale. A wave score is the mean of the valid items, computed only when at least 75% of the items have a valid response. Documented non-answer codes are recoded to missing before scoring. The item table shows the keying listed in the guide (reverse_guide) and the keying applied (reverse); see decision D17 in the repository’s decision log.

Psychometrics

Reliability is computed locally from the item-level data and saved as an aggregate-only file (config/psychometrics_results.csv, produced by R/08_psychometrics.R); this report reads only that file. Statistics use respondents with a valid answer on every item of the wave (complete cases). Alpha is Cronbach’s alpha. Omega is McDonald’s omega total from a one-factor model, so it assumes a single dimension; this is provisional for right-wing authoritarianism (possibly three facets) and social dominance orientation (possibly two), pending a multi-factor check. The last column is the lowest corrected item-rest correlation.

Longitudinal report

Can scores be compared across waves?

For this construct, the same 5 items with the same wording appear in every wave, and the response scale is 1 to 7 in all of them (one range for all items). Documented non-answer codes: none. The repository’s validation checks (decision log D01 to D16) found no differences in wording, response options or range between waves. Wave scores are therefore on the same metric, and mean-level comparison across waves is defensible. This establishes that the scores are comparable on paper; it does not show measurement invariance, which would need a longitudinal factor model and is listed as a limitation.

Mean change and rank-order stability

Estimates use every respondent with a score at both waves (available-case). Intervals are 95% confidence intervals on the paired differences. Mixed-model estimate of change

As a complement to the pairwise contrasts, a linear mixed model estimates the mean at each wave with a random intercept per respondent: score ~ wave (factor) + (1 | respondent), fitted by REML with Satterthwaite degrees of freedom. All available observations are used, so no balanced panel is assumed. The table gives the wave 1 mean and the change from wave 1 at each later wave with 95% confidence intervals. The intraclass correlation (ICC) is the share of variance due to stable differences between respondents. The contrasts are unadjusted for multiple comparisons, so a few intervals excluding 0 are expected by chance. Estimates are associations in observed scores and do not address measurement invariance (see the comparability statement above).

Departures and limitations

  • Departures from the public PPBS reports. This report adds a longitudinal section, suppresses cells below 10, and omits minimum and maximum values. Sections not yet built are marked as such in the text.
  • Keying. The guide lists reverse-keyed items for some constructs, but the extract appears already recoded, so no reversal is applied (decision D17, to be confirmed with the research team). Agreement tendency cannot be separated from substantive responding where a construct has no reverse-keyed items, as is the case for right-wing authoritarianism.
  • Short forms. Constructs with few items (belief in a dangerous world, competitive worldview) have limited reliability and content coverage.
