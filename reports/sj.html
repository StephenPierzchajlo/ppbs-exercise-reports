System justification

Study characteristics

This report covers System justification: 500 respondents linked by exercise_id across 5 waves (1 to 5), measured with 8 items on a 1 to 7 response scale. A wave score is the mean of the valid items, computed only when at least 75% of the items have a valid response. Documented non-answer codes are recoded to missing before scoring. The item table shows the keying listed in the guide (reverse_guide) and the keying applied (reverse); see decision D17 in the repository’s decision log.

Longitudinal report

Can scores be compared across waves?

For this construct, the same 8 items with the same wording appear in every wave, and the response scale is 1 to 7 in all of them (one range for all items). Documented non-answer codes: none. The repository’s validation checks (decision log D01 to D16) found no differences in wording, response options or range between waves. Wave scores are therefore on the same metric, and mean-level comparison across waves is defensible. This establishes that the scores are comparable on paper; it does not show measurement invariance, which would need a longitudinal factor model and is listed as a limitation.

Mean change and rank-order stability

Estimates use every respondent with a score at both waves (available-case). Intervals are 95% confidence intervals on the paired differences. They are estimates with uncertainty, not significance tests, because many wave pairs are shown. dz is the mean change in units of the SD of the change scores.

Departures and limitations

  • Departures from the public PPBS reports. This report adds a longitudinal section, suppresses cells below 10, and omits minimum and maximum values. Sections not yet built are marked as such in the text.
  • Keying. Mixed keying across constructs means agreement tendency can affect constructs differently (decision D15).
  • Short forms. Constructs with few items (belief in a dangerous world, competitive worldview) have limited reliability and content coverage.

Reproducibility
